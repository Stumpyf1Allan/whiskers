# Whiskers 2.0: one app for the whole picture

Version 2.0 turns Whiskers from an investment tracker into one app for everything a
household owns and owes: investments, cash in any currency, the home and its mortgage,
pensions, and a year-by-year plan for retirement. It keeps everything 1.x does, and the
budgeting from Mittens & Pence can be switched on for anyone who wants it.

It is still a tool for keeping to your own plan. It is not financial advice.

## The tabs

| Tab | What it holds |
|---|---|
| **Overall** | Net worth (what you own less what you owe), a health tile for each tab, the big picture in plain English, the dates coming up in the next year, and the **plan review**. |
| **Investments** | Everything 1.x had: Summary, Holdings, Allocation, Market watch, Markets, Activity, plus **Import** (drop a file, the statements folder, the monthly reminder). |
| **Budget** | Off unless switched on in Settings. Bank statements, spending by category, monthly budgets. Only counted on Overall when it is on. |
| **Mortgage** | The balance running down, interest against capital, the date the fixed rate ends, what an overpayment would save, what a new rate would cost. |
| **Retirement** | A plan one row a year to the end of retirement, **Pensions** (final salary, pots, annuities, South African retirement annuities) and the **State Pension**. |

Mortgage and Retirement can be switched off in Settings. Settings is the gear at the top
right; Help (bottom of the sidebar) opens an email to Allan with the version and the
screen, and nothing about money.

On a phone the tabs move to the bottom of the screen and each tab's screens run along the
top.

## The plan review (Overall › Plan review)

Eight parts, saved as each question is answered, so it can be done over several sittings:

1. **You**: date of birth, where you live (Scotland has its own tax bands), who shares
   your finances, how long the plan should run, how secure your work is, when you'd like
   to stop and whether you'd cut your hours first, where you'll live in retirement.
2. **Your goals**: essential and extra spending a month, in today's money (with the
   Retirement Living Standards for comparison), whether extras fall from 75, the mortgage,
   big costs ahead, money needed within five years, and leaving money to family.
3. **Your safety net**: emergency fund, debts, life cover, income protection, will,
   South African will, lasting powers of attorney, pension nominations.
4. **Income for life**: pensions (added on the Pensions screen), the State Pension
   forecast and record, salary, workplace pension contributions, monthly saving, other
   income.
5. **Tax and wrappers**: employer matching, Self Assessment, the ISA allowance.
6. **Risk**: what you would do in a fall, and what you actually did in 2020 or 2022.
7. **Beliefs and worries**: themes you'd like a stake in, what worries you, the one
   market force to watch, single companies or funds only, time, exclusions, UK bias,
   income.
8. **What you hold**: the **X-Ray** (after Morningstar's): asset mix, regions,
   currencies, the themes your money rides on once funds are looked through, the biggest
   companies you own counting funds, single companies against the world index, charges.

Answers the Retirement tab also uses are stored once, so the two never disagree. The
app's own defaults never count as answers.

**See the plan it builds** shows the draft, and changes nothing until **Use the ticked
parts**:

- **How much risk**: three tests (Bogleheads, after Larry Swedroe): the risk you are
  *willing* to take, *able* to take, and *need* to take. The plan takes the lower of
  willing and able, as the FCA expects advisers to; a real sale in a past fall counts for
  more than a bold answer now; if the goals need less, it offers less.
- **Your target mix**: sleeves (kinds of investment, never named funds), with the bucket
  approach for anyone already drawing on their money.
- **What to keep an eye on**: the themes your money rides on, plus what you said
  matters, with the biggest as your **main watch**. That becomes the dial on Overall and
  Summary, and the lights and signals on Market watch. Someone whose money rides on gold
  and gilts gets gold and interest-rate lights; someone with a lot in technology funds
  gets the AI watch. A copy that never chooses keeps the original AI watch.
- **Your rules**, **your plan for a fall**, stress tests (shares down a third, rates up a
  point, the rand down a fifth, and each watched theme's own worst fall of the last five
  years replayed on today's holdings), and **things worth doing**, with dates: filling a
  National Insurance gap before its deadline, pension nominations before April 2027, the
  ISA allowance, the cash ISA cap from April 2027, and so on.

Using the plan sorts any holding not yet in a sleeve into the one its name or kind points
to (a world fund to the core, a gilt fund to gilts) and says so; the Allocation screen
shows them all to check.

**The written plan** is everything decided, in one page, to print or save. **Download the
review pack** saves a plain-text file with the plan, every holding and the X-Ray, to hand
to an adviser (or Claude) for a second opinion; read it before sharing it.

## The retirement plan

One row a year, from now to the end of the plan, **in today's money**: £20,000 in 2045
means what £20,000 buys now. Each year:

- **Coming in**: pay (less National Insurance and pension contributions) until you stop,
  or cut your hours; each pension from its own age; the State Pension from State Pension
  age (part of a year in the first year); other income.
- **Going out**: essentials and extras (extras can fall from 75), the mortgage until it
  clears, big one-off costs, and income tax worked out with the real bands (Scotland's
  own), thresholds frozen until April 2031 and then rising with prices.
- **When there's a shortfall**, a pot pays, in the order chosen: cash, then the general
  account, ISAs, money abroad and pensions (or pensions first, or pensions up to the
  basic-rate band each year). An emergency fund is kept back until last.
- **When there's money left over**, it is saved: into the ISA up to the allowance, then
  as cash.

What it assumes, all changeable under *Your figures and the assumptions*: shares grow 4%
a year above inflation, bonds 1.5%, cash 0.5%; your own investments grow at a rate worked
out from what they actually hold, less their charges; a pension pot nobody has described
grows 3%; inflation 2.5% for anything fixed in pounds. A final-salary pension taken early
is reduced (5% a year unless its scheme says otherwise); a quarter of a pot comes out tax
free, within the £268,275 lump sum allowance. When a mortgage's fixed rate ends, the plan
assumes a new fix at today's average (6.00% for five years on 5 October 2026); the stress
test shows staying on the lender's standard rate instead. A UK State Pension paid in South
Africa is frozen.

Alongside: **Try a change** (stop later, spend less on extras, save more now, the order
of withdrawals: saved only when asked), **To make it last** (how big one change would need
to be), **If things go badly** (a fall of a third the year you stop, inflation two points
higher for five years, the standard rate, living to 100, stopping two years early or late,
and each watched theme's worst fall), **The growth the plan needs**, the orders compared
by tax, what is left and inheritance tax (pensions count from 6 April 2027), and every
year in full.

Not modelled: tax on interest and dividends outside ISAs, South African tax, care costs,
gifts and trusts. Rough figures in today's money, not a forecast.

## Getting files in

- **Import a file** (Investments › Import, or drop a file anywhere on the window):
  Freetrade's activity export, Trading 212's CSV, and the holdings files from Hargreaves
  Lansdown, AJ Bell, interactive investor and Vanguard Investor. The file is read and
  shown first: what it is, its rows, the platform it belongs to. Nothing goes in until
  *Import it*. Importing an overlapping file never counts anything twice. With the Budget
  on, bank statements too (CSV, OFX, QIF, Excel, and PDF where the PDF reader is
  installed).
- **The statements folder** (Documents › "Whiskers - Statements to import" unless
  changed): put each month's downloads in it and press Refresh. A file goes in by itself
  only when its layout is known *and* its filename names one platform; anything else
  stays put and is listed with the reason. Imported files move into "Filed". Nothing is
  ever deleted, and a renamed copy of a file already in is recognised by its contents.
- **The monthly reminder**: a strip across the top on the day chosen, until that month's
  files are in, and a calendar file (.ics) that repeats every month in Outlook, Google or
  Apple calendars.
- **Find your platform or account** (Settings, Import or the Overall checklist): over
  270 UK and South African banks, building societies, platforms and pension providers,
  each saying honestly how it keeps up to date: *connects automatically* (Trading 212,
  Monzo), *download a file* (with where the file is), or *type it in*.

## Money in other currencies

A bank account can be in rand (or euros or dollars). It is kept as the bank reports it and
converted at each day's rate wherever pounds are added up; with no rate yet it is left out
and the screen says so, never counted one for one. South African accounts are checked
against CoDI's R100,000 per bank. The X-Ray counts rand as a currency risk for anyone
retiring in the UK.

## Trying 2.0 without touching your own Whiskers

Double-click **`Try Whiskers 2.0 (test copy).bat`**. It runs from the source folder and
keeps its data in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Whiskers 2.0 test`, so your own Whiskers, its data and
the family's copies are untouched. Delete that folder to start the test again. Settings ›
*Sample household* loads a made-up household (investments, a home and mortgage, two
pensions, savings in pounds and rand, retirement basics) and removes it again in one
click.

- New engine modules: `engine/retirement.py`, `pensions.py`, `statepension.py`, `uktax.py`,
  `ukrules.py` (every dated figure, with its source), `mortgage.py` (from Mittens &
  Pence), `overall.py`, `builder.py`, `xray.py`, `classify.py`, `themes.py`; the Budget in
  `folio/budget/`; files in `folio/importers/`, `folio/inbox.py`, `folio/reminders.py`; the
  catalogue in `folio/institutions/`.
- Routes for all of it are in `web/routes2.py`; the screens are split across
  `web/static/*.js` (one file per tab), loaded by `index.html` and started on
  `DOMContentLoaded`.
- `tests/test_mega.py` covers the new parts; `WHISKERS_FAKE_MARKET=1` invents prices for
  testing offline.
- The data folder, the keychain service name and the update address are unchanged, so a
  1.x copy updates in place and keeps everything.
